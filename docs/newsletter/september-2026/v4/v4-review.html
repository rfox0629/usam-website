02  /  The Model in Action

What begins at a table
can keep growing.

Kitchen Table Gospel is the model, and it is not complicated. Real homes. Real tables. A meal, an open Bible, honest conversation, and prayer out loud.

What makes it discipleship is not the evening itself. It is what keeps going afterward.

Kitchen Table Gospel // Minnesota

 

From the Table

One evening,
and what came after.

One evening around a table, a woman joined us for conversation, worship, and prayer. Later she told us it was not one dramatic moment that stayed with her. She had sensed God's presence through the whole evening, and had begun to recognise what was standing between her and Him.

As music played, she took time to write and reflect. She said it helped her see both her own heart and God's heart more clearly. In the days that followed she described more peace, more time in Scripture, and a growing desire to care intentionally for her family and her marriage.

What encouraged us most was her reminder that the gathering was only the beginning. We have kept reaching out, praying, and making time for her. This is the kind of ministry we hope to see at tables across the country: honest conversation, room to respond to God, and a relationship that continues after the evening ends.

 

Men are gathering.

Two men's groups are also meeting consistently. They open Scripture together, pray, build real accountability, and keep walking through actual life with one another.

It is ordinary and it is working.

Men's discipleship // Two groups meeting weekly

2three2 — Run. Pray. Pursue.

Your table is enough.

Invite someone in. Open Scripture together. Pray out loud, even if it is short. Then follow up during the week.

That is the whole model, and it is the one thing we would ask you to try.

Gather. Learn. Confess. Encourage. Multiply.

03  /  The Tool Being Prepared

Helping discipleship
continue.

We are getting close to a broader launch of the Discipleship Operating System. It has been in limited use while we build it, shaped by the very work you have just read about.

It is made to help disciple makers carry the people, the prayers, and the next steps they promised, so discipleship keeps going between the gatherings.

Technology cannot replace a conversation, a prayer, or a relationship. We only hope it makes it easier to keep showing up for someone, and to help them do the same for someone else.
